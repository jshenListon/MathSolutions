NCEA Level 3 Calculus Solutions

Step-by-step worked solutions for Locus Equations (Complex Numbers).

Locus Equations

Question 1

Find the Cartesian equation of the locus described by $|z+i|=2|z-5i|$ in the form $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=k^2$.

Step 1: Substitute $z = x + iy$

$$ |x + iy + i| = 2|x + iy - 5i| $$ $$ |x + i(y+1)| = 2|x + i(y-5)| $$

Step 2: Apply the modulus formula $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ and square both sides

$$ \sqrt{x^2 + (y+1)^2} = 2\sqrt{x^2 + (y-5)^2} $$ $$ x^2 + (y+1)^2 = 4[x^2 + (y-5)^2] $$

Step 3: Expand and group like terms

$$ x^2 + y^2 + 2y + 1 = 4(x^2 + y^2 - 10y + 25) $$ $$ x^2 + y^2 + 2y + 1 = 4x^2 + 4y^2 - 40y + 100 $$

Move everything to the right side:

$$ 0 = 3x^2 + 3y^2 - 42y + 99 $$ $$ x^2 + y^2 - 14y + 33 = 0 $$

Step 4: Complete the square for y

$$ x^2 + (y^2 - 14y + 49) - 49 + 33 = 0 $$ $$ x^2 + (y-7)^2 - 16 = 0 $$
Final Equation: $x^2 + (y-7)^2 = 16$    *(Circle with center (0,7) and radius 4)*

Question 2

The straight line with equation $y=mx-1$, where $m$ is a real constant and $m>0$, is a tangent to the locus described by $|z-2+i|=\sqrt{3}$. Find the Cartesian equation of the locus AND the value of $m$.

Step 1: Find the Cartesian equation of the locus

$$ |x + iy - 2 + i| = \sqrt{3} $$ $$ |(x-2) + i(y+1)| = \sqrt{3} $$ $$ (x-2)^2 + (y+1)^2 = 3 $$

This is a circle centered at $(2, -1)$ with radius $\sqrt{3}$.

Step 2: Substitute the line equation into the circle equation

Substitute $y = mx - 1$ into the circle equation:

$$ (x-2)^2 + (mx - 1 + 1)^2 = 3 $$ $$ (x-2)^2 + (mx)^2 = 3 $$ $$ x^2 - 4x + 4 + m^2x^2 - 3 = 0 $$ $$ (1+m^2)x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0 $$

Step 3: Use the Discriminant for tangency

Because the line is a tangent, it intersects the circle at exactly one point, meaning the discriminant ($\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$) must equal 0.

$$ a = 1+m^2, \quad b = -4, \quad c = 1 $$ $$ (-4)^2 - 4(1+m^2)(1) = 0 $$ $$ 16 - 4 - 4m^2 = 0 $$ $$ 12 = 4m^2 \implies m^2 = 3 $$

Since we are given $m > 0$, we take the positive root.

Cartesian Equation: $(x-2)^2 + (y+1)^2 = 3$
Value of m: $m = \sqrt{3}$

Question 3

The locus of a complex number z is described by $|z-1-7i|=2|z-4-4i|$. The complex number $u=3+di$ lies on this locus. Find the Cartesian equation of the locus of z, giving your answer in the form $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=k$ and also find the complex number(s) $u$.

Step 1: Cartesian Equation of the Locus

$$ |x-1 + i(y-7)| = 2|x-4 + i(y-4)| $$ $$ (x-1)^2 + (y-7)^2 = 4[(x-4)^2 + (y-4)^2] $$ $$ x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 - 14y + 49 = 4(x^2 - 8x + 16 + y^2 - 8y + 16) $$ $$ x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 14y + 50 = 4x^2 + 4y^2 - 32x - 32y + 128 $$ $$ 3x^2 + 3y^2 - 30x - 18y + 78 = 0 $$

Divide by 3:

$$ x^2 + y^2 - 10x - 6y + 26 = 0 $$ $$ (x-5)^2 - 25 + (y-3)^2 - 9 + 26 = 0 $$ $$ (x-5)^2 + (y-3)^2 = 8 $$

Step 2: Substitute $u = 3+di$ to find $d$

This means $x=3$ and $y=d$ must satisfy the equation of the circle.

$$ (3-5)^2 + (d-3)^2 = 8 $$ $$ (-2)^2 + (d-3)^2 = 8 $$ $$ 4 + (d-3)^2 = 8 \implies (d-3)^2 = 4 $$ $$ d-3 = \pm 2 $$ $$ d = 5 \quad \text{or} \quad d = 1 $$
Cartesian Equation: $(x-5)^2 + (y-3)^2 = 8$
Complex Numbers: $u = 3+5i$ or $u = 3+i$

Question 4

The locus described by $|z-i|=|z+1|$ is a straight line. Find the gradient of that line. Justify your answer.

Step 1: Algebraic Justification

$$ |x + i(y-1)| = |x+1 + iy| $$ $$ x^2 + (y-1)^2 = (x+1)^2 + y^2 $$ $$ x^2 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = x^2 + 2x + 1 + y^2 $$ $$ -2y = 2x $$ $$ y = -x $$

Step 2: Geometric Justification

The equation $|z-i|=|z+1|$ geometrically represents the locus of all points $z$ that are equidistant from the points $(0,1)$ and $(-1,0)$. This forms the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining those two points.

Gradient: $m = -1$
Justification: The line is $y = -x$, which clearly has a gradient of -1.

Question 5

The locus of a complex number z is described by $|z-5i|-|z+5i|=4$. Find the Cartesian equation of the locus of z, giving your answer in the form $ay^2-bx^2=k$, where a, b, k are constants.

Step 1: Set up the algebraic equation

$$ \sqrt{x^2 + (y-5)^2} - \sqrt{x^2 + (y+5)^2} = 4 $$

Isolate one of the square roots to square both sides:

$$ \sqrt{x^2 + (y-5)^2} = 4 + \sqrt{x^2 + (y+5)^2} $$

Step 2: Square both sides

$$ x^2 + (y-5)^2 = 16 + 8\sqrt{x^2 + (y+5)^2} + [x^2 + (y+5)^2] $$ $$ x^2 + y^2 - 10y + 25 = 16 + 8\sqrt{x^2 + (y+5)^2} + x^2 + y^2 + 10y + 25 $$

Cancel $x^2, y^2, 25$ from both sides and group terms:

$$ -20y - 16 = 8\sqrt{x^2 + (y+5)^2} $$

Divide by 4 to simplify before squaring again:

$$ -5y - 4 = 2\sqrt{x^2 + (y+5)^2} $$

Step 3: Square both sides again

$$ (-5y - 4)^2 = 4[x^2 + (y+5)^2] $$ $$ 25y^2 + 40y + 16 = 4(x^2 + y^2 + 10y + 25) $$ $$ 25y^2 + 40y + 16 = 4x^2 + 4y^2 + 40y + 100 $$

Cancel the $40y$ and group the $x$ and $y$ variables:

$$ 21y^2 - 4x^2 = 84 $$
Final Equation: $21y^2 - 4x^2 = 84$    *(This is a Hyperbola)*
